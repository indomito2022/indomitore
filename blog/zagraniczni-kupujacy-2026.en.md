Local buyers in Spain are buying fewer homes, but foreigners are buying more than ever. In Q3 2026, international buyers accounted for 16.05% of all home purchases in Spain, the highest share on record.

## A record share in a slowing market

According to the latest report from Spain's Land Registrars (Registradores de España), 164,148 homes were sold in Spain between July and September 2026. That is 5.9% fewer than a year earlier, the third quarterly decline in a row and the lowest figure in nine quarters.

Foreign buyers moved the other way. They completed more than 26,300 purchases, around 11% more than in Q3 2025, and their share of the market rose from 13.58% to 16.05%. More than half of them (54.7%) were citizens of other EU countries.

In short, the domestic market is cooling while international demand keeps growing. That gap is what shapes prices on the coast right now.

The British are still the largest group of foreign buyers in Spain, years after Brexit. Moroccan buyers have moved into second place, ahead of the Dutch and Germans.

The most striking change is further down the list. Romanian, Italian and Polish buyers now purchase almost as many homes as German and French buyers, who for decades dominated the coast. Polish buyers alone bought 3,358 homes in the first nine months of 2026, their best start to a year ever.

## Where they buy

Foreign demand is concentrated on the coast and the islands. The province of Alicante, home to the Costa Blanca, had the highest share in the country: 46.46% of all purchases there went to foreign buyers. In other words, almost one home in two.

Málaga, which covers most of the Costa del Sol, came second with 38.83%, up from 31.84% a year earlier. Sales in the province fell by 6.8%, yet foreigners bought around 14% more homes there.

By region, the Balearic Islands lead with 37.2%, followed by the Valencian Community with 30.4%. Everywhere else, foreign buyers account for less than 30% of sales.

## What it means for buyers

Fewer sales have not brought prices down. The average price in Spain reached a record €2,512 per m² in Q3 2026, 9.1% higher than a year earlier. Growth is slowing, though: prices rose 1% on the previous quarter, compared with 2.4% in Q2.

As long as international demand stays this strong, buyers should not count on falling prices in the most popular coastal areas. What they can count on is more time to compare. Sales are taking longer, and buyers are more selective than a year ago.
