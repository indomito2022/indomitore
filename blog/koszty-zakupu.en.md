The asking price is not the whole story. When you buy an apartment or a house in Spain, you need to add taxes and fees on top. Here are all the purchase costs in one place, with the rates in force in 2026 on the Costa Blanca and the Costa Cálida.

> In short: a safe budget for buying costs is around **14% of the price for a new build** and **13% for a resale property**.

## New build: VAT (IVA) and stamp duty (AJD)

When you buy from a developer, you pay two taxes:

- **IVA (Spanish VAT): 10%** of the price of the home. It is added to every payment to the developer. A parking space or storage room bought together with the home is also taxed at 10%; bought separately, at 21%.
- **AJD (stamp duty):** in the Valencian Community it is **1.4%** since 1 June 2026 (previously 1.5%). In the Region of Murcia it is **1.5%**.

In total, taxes on a new build on the Costa Blanca come to **11.4% of the price**. Current regional rates are published by the [Valencian Tax Agency (ATV)](https://atv.gva.es) and the [Murcia Regional Tax Agency](https://agenciatributaria.carm.es); VAT rates are set by the [Spanish VAT Act](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1992-28740).

## Tourist apartment for holiday rental: 21% VAT you can reclaim

**Tourist apartments** are a different case: units in complexes intended for holiday rental. Buying one from a developer means **21% VAT** instead of 10% (plus AJD).

The good news: if you buy such an apartment **to let it to holidaymakers**, you can **claim that VAT back**. The refund is usually requested in the return for the last quarter of the year, filed in January, so you get the money **in the year after the purchase**. Conditions:

- you register the rental activity with the [Spanish Tax Agency](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es) (form 036/037) and file quarterly VAT returns,
- the rental is subject to VAT: you provide hotel-type services (cleaning during the stay, linen changes, reception, etc.) and charge guests VAT, usually 10%,
- the apartment stays in the rental business: there is a 10-year adjustment period, so if you stop renting it out earlier or start living in it, part of the deducted VAT has to be paid back.

> In practice: on a €200,000 apartment you pay €42,000 VAT on purchase and, if you meet the conditions, you can recover it. Before buying, go through the details with your lawyer or tax adviser.

## Resale property: transfer tax (ITP)

Buying from a private seller, you pay no VAT or AJD, only **ITP** (property transfer tax):

| Region | ITP rate 2026 |
|---|---|
| Valencian Community (Costa Blanca) | **9%** – up to €1 million |
| Valencian Community, above €1 million | **11%** on the full value |
| Region of Murcia (Costa Cálida) | **7.75%** |

In the Valencian Community the rate fell from 10% to 9% on 1 June 2026. ITP is charged on **the higher of two values**: the price in the deed or the official *valor de referencia*, which you can look up on the [Spanish Cadastre (Catastro) website](https://www.sedecatastro.gob.es).

Reduced rates exist for some Spanish residents (young first-time buyers of a main home, large families, etc.), but they usually do not apply to a holiday home.

## Notary and land registry

These fees apply to both new and resale properties:

- **Notary:** around €600–1,200. Fees follow official scales and depend on the price and on the **number of pages in the deed**. More about the notary's role at the [General Council of Spanish Notaries](https://www.notariado.org).
- **Land registry** (*Registro de la Propiedad*): around €400–800.

## Lawyer

For a foreign buyer, a lawyer is essential. They:

- check the legal status of the property, the contracts and the developer's or seller's documents,
- **file the tax returns on your behalf** and pay the taxes,
- **register the deed at the Land Registry**,
- on a resale: handle **the settlements with the seller** and **transfer the utilities** into your name,
- usually also **translate at the notary** – normally the lawyer does this, unless you prefer a sworn translator.

Cost: lawyers' fees vary. They are often **1% of the price + VAT**, but many firms have minimum or fixed fees, usually **€1,000–1,500 + VAT**.

## NIE and a Spanish bank account

- **NIE** (foreigner identification number): it is not needed to sign the deed itself, but **you need it to pay the taxes** and register the purchase, so it is best to get it early ([Spanish National Police](https://www.policia.es) or a Spanish consulate).
- **Spanish bank account:** not mandatory, but recommended for paying taxes, utilities and community fees.

## Mortgage: what extra costs to expect

If you buy with a mortgage, **you need to budget for higher costs**. Since 2019 the bank covers part of the set-up costs (stamp duty on the loan, notary and registration of the mortgage), but you still pay for the **valuation** (*tasación*, around €300–600), any bank fees and the insurance the bank requires.

## Property insurance

Among the recommended costs is **home insurance**. If you want to **rent the property out**, insurance is required.

## Example: a €250,000 home on the Costa Blanca

![Calculating the costs of buying property in Spain](koszty-zakupu-kalkulator.jpg)

| Cost | New build | Resale |
|---|---|---|
| IVA 10% | €25,000 | – |
| AJD 1.4% | €3,500 | – |
| ITP 9% | – | €22,500 |
| Notary and registry | approx. €1,500 | approx. €1,500 |
| Lawyer (€2,500 + VAT) | €3,025 | €3,025 |
| **Total** | **approx. €33,000 (13.2%)** | **approx. €27,000 (10.8%)** |

On top of this come insurance and, with a mortgage, its costs. That is why a safe budget is **14% for a new build** and **13% for a resale**.

## How not to overpay

1. **Budget 14% from day one** for a new build and 13% for a resale.
2. **On a resale, check the official reference value** before negotiating the price.
3. **Work with an agent who knows the market.** A good agent will show you the real costs from the start and present properties you won't overpay for, because they know what homes in each area are really worth.
